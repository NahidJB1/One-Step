Affordable Countries to Study Abroad: Compare 10 Destinations by Total Cost

A country with low tuition can still need substantial savings for rent, deposits and visa evidence. Start with the qualification you want, then compare the complete cost and your eligibility to study there.

The quick answer

There is no universal cheapest destination. Compare the same study level, nationality category, teaching language and funding status. These ten destinations are research options, not a price ranking or a visa-success league table.

Scholarship and visa rules belong to a particular call or route. A government funding announcement does not prove that an application window is still open, and a national tuition range does not price your individual course.

Build a comparison that answers your real question

First choose your study level and subject. Then collect two or three actual programme offers in the destinations you are considering. Record the language of teaching and assessment, qualification recognition, duration, tuition for your nationality and the source date.

Keep the original currency next to each source figure. If you convert to your home currency, record the exchange rate and date separately and leave room for changes. Do not compare a monthly living estimate in one destination with a whole-year total in another.

Proof of funds is not another tuition fee

A blocked account or other accepted financial evidence can demonstrate money available for living expenses. It is not automatically a government charge. Understand when you can access the money and whether it is already included in your living budget.

Avoid double counting

If a living-cost reserve is also used as financial evidence, do not add the same money twice to your total. However, check access restrictions: you may still need separate cash for an initial housing deposit, tuition deadline or travel.

Germany’s EUR11,904 figure above is the official 2026 benchmark for the specified admitted-student route. It is not a rule for all European countries or every German visa category. Each destination needs its own current checklist.

Compare funded and self-funded plans separately

Make one budget without a scholarship and another using only a confirmed award. For each award, record whether it covers tuition, accommodation, insurance, travel or a stipend, and when support begins. A living allowance paid after arrival does not pay an earlier deposit.

Keep deadlines in a separate column. A closed call can help you prepare documents, but its dates and conditions should not be presented as an upcoming opportunity. Also check academic renewal rules and whether changing course would end the award.

Questions students ask

Which destination offers guaranteed student visas?

None is presented here as guaranteeing a visa. Decisions depend on the applicant and the current rules of the relevant authorities.

Does free tuition mean I can study without savings?

No. Living expenses, financial evidence, travel, insurance and other charges can remain payable. A scholarship may also exclude some of these costs.
