Cost of Living in Malaysia for International Students

Tuition is only one part of the cost of studying abroad. Build a budget for rent, food, transport and the money you need before your first class.

The quick answer

There is no single monthly amount that fits every student. Get a room quote near your actual campus, then add everyday spending. The RM1,800 monthly example below is a planning exercise, not a survey average or a guaranteed minimum.

What do official estimates say?

Education Malaysia publishes broad accommodation estimates in USD: US$50–150 per month on campus and US$100–300 off campus. It also gives an indicative utilities range of US$40–65 per month. These are general estimates, not room offers or a quote for a particular city. Source: Education Malaysia: cost of living.

We retain the source currency rather than silently converting it. For your own budget, use the rent and bills quoted in RM, and check the exchange rate and transfer charges on the day you send money.

An example monthly budget in RM

Illustrative assumptions: one student, a RM700 room, everyday meals, modest local travel and shared bills. Replace every line with your own quotes. Tuition, flights, deposits, insurance and visa costs are excluded.

Reduce costs without losing track of essentials

Compare the combined cost of rent and commuting: a cheaper room far from campus may need more paid transport. Ask whether the room has air-conditioning metering, cooking facilities and a minimum contract. Check whether a campus shuttle operates during evenings and breaks.

Keep a record of your first month’s spending and revise the plan. Food delivery, frequent ride-hailing and setup purchases can make the first month look very different from a normal study month. Agree with your family on how much is available before committing to a room.

Do not rely on part-time earnings to close a funding gap. Education Malaysia describes work as restricted to approved settings and periods, with prior Immigration permission. Ask your international office about your circumstances. Education Malaysia: student FAQs.

Questions students ask

Does RM1,800 include tuition?

No. The example covers only the five monthly living categories shown. Add tuition, visa and insurance costs, deposits, travel and a separate reserve.

Is a scholarship allowance enough to live on?

Compare the award’s actual payment amount and timing against your own rent and spending. Funding can be paid periodically, so a monthly allowance description does not mean money arrives every month.
